Common Questions

FAQs on Mediation

What is Mediation?

add

Mediation is a voluntary, confidential process in which a neutral third party — the mediator — helps both parties reach a mutually acceptable resolution. Unlike a courtroom, the mediator doesn't decide the outcome. You do.

The mediator facilitates conversation, helps identify common ground, and guides both parties toward a durable agreement that reflects the needs and priorities of your family — not a judge's determination.

Sessions are private, and anything discussed in mediation cannot be used against you in court proceedings if the mediation is unsuccessful.

Half-Days vs Full-Days vs 2-Hour Blocks

add

2-Hour Block — Best for single-issue disputes or parties who are close to agreement and need focused guidance on one specific topic such as a parenting schedule or a single asset.

Half-Day (4 hours) — Ideal for moderately complex matters involving multiple issues where the parties are reasonably aligned but need a structured space to finalize details.

Full-Day (8 hours) — Recommended for comprehensive settlements involving property division, custody arrangements, child support, and financial planning. Full-day sessions allow time to work through complexity without the pressure of running out of time.

What are the Possible Outcomes?

add

Mediation can result in a full settlement agreement covering all outstanding issues — the most common and ideal outcome. Most mediated agreements are signed the same day and can be submitted directly to the court.

It may also produce a partial agreement on some issues, with remaining points addressed through other means. This is still valuable — it narrows the dispute and reduces the cost of any further proceedings.

In some cases, parties reach an impasse. Even then, the process often clarifies each party's priorities and reduces conflict in subsequent proceedings.

How Much Does Mediation Cost?

add

Fees vary based on session type and are typically shared equally between both parties. Please contact us directly for current rates.

In most cases, mediation is significantly less expensive than contested litigation — and considerably faster. A single full-day mediation often costs less than one month of active litigation, and resolves matters that could otherwise take years to conclude in court.

We are happy to discuss fees transparently during an initial consultation.
